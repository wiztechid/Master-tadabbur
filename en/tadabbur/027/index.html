Quran 31:18: Do Not Turn Away from People in Arrogance

SESSION 027 • LUQMAN 31:18 • THEMATIC/NON-LINEAR

Do Not Walk in Arrogance

Humility, arrogance, social manners.

🌙 Today's Key Verse

وَلَا تُصَعِّرْ خَدَّكَ لِلنَّاسِ وَلَا تَمْشِ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ مَرَحًا ۖ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ لَا يُحِبُّ كُلَّ مُخْتَالٍۢ فَخُورٍۢ
Wa lā tuṣa‘‘ir khaddaka lin-nāsi wa lā tamsyi fil-arḍi maraḥā. Innallāha lā yuḥibbu kulla mukhtālin fakhūr.
LUQMAN 31:18 — M. Pickthall: Turn not thy cheek in scorn toward folk, nor walk with pertness in the land. Lo! Allah loveth not each braggart boaster.
English Qur'an translation reference: M. Pickthall (public-domain translation). Arabic text and transliteration are identical to the Indonesian edition. Transliteration is a reading aid and does not replace tajwid or talaqqi.

📚 Tafsir Notes

Do not look down on people

Ibn Kathir explains the turning of the face as contemptuous arrogance; advantages do not grant permission to belittle others.

Arrogance in conduct

Al-Tabari relates walking with maraḥ to boastful, self-exalting behaviour.

Boundary

Humility does not erase firmness, competence, or legitimate authority. What is condemned is rejecting truth and looking down on people.

🤲 Authentic Hadith

Sahih Muslim 91 defines arrogance as rejecting truth and looking down on people.

🌍 Factual Context

Fact from revelation: the verse prohibits contemptuous and arrogant conduct.

Lesson/analogy: status, knowledge, experience, and wealth can test character when correction becomes hard to hear.

Boundary: one gesture cannot reveal a person's heart; this is primarily a mirror for self-examination.

🪞 Arrogance that does not look like showing off

Everyday arrogance can be subtler than displaying wealth or status. It can appear when we cannot accept correction from someone younger, treat another person's work as unimportant, interrupt because we assume we already know, or speak differently to service workers than to people we consider influential. The issue is not merely posture; it is how we position ourselves in relation to other people.

Self-examination is therefore more useful when it focuses on observable behavior instead of only asking the abstract question, “Am I arrogant?”

🧭 Hidden Arrogance Check

Ask four questions. Correction: do I judge feedback by its substance or by the status of the person giving it? Status: does my courtesy change around people who cannot benefit me? Speaking space: do I let others finish? Error: when I am shown to be wrong, do I correct the information or mainly protect my image?

This checklist is not a tool for diagnosing other people as “arrogant.” It is deliberately aimed inward because similar outward behavior can arise from different contexts and intentions.

⚖️ Humility is not pretending you have no ability

A person can acknowledge expertise, accept leadership responsibility, or state a position firmly without belittling others. Humility also does not require allowing errors to continue simply to appear gentle. At work, a leader may still need standards and correction; the question is whether correction preserves human dignity and addresses the problem rather than feeding a sense of superiority.

Low self-worth is not the goal either. Recognizing blessings and abilities while remembering one's limits is healthier than pretending to be incapable.

🚧 This session's territory

Session 027 focuses on contempt for people and hidden superiority in relationships. Session 043 has a different territory: measured conduct, walking, and lowering the voice around Luqman 31:19. We do not use one outward behavior as certain proof of another person's heart, and we do not claim to know a person's standing before Allah.

🗣️ Test your conduct when correction stings

One of the most useful moments for testing humility is when feedback makes us uncomfortable. Before responding, separate three things: the substance of the correction, the way it was delivered, and the reaction of our ego. Poor delivery can be challenged without automatically rejecting information that is true. Likewise, politely delivered criticism is not automatically correct merely because it sounds pleasant.

Try pausing before defending yourself. Restate the core feedback in your own words, ask for an example if it is unclear, and acknowledge the part that is genuinely right. If another part is mistaken, answer its substance without belittling the messenger. This turns “accepting truth” into conduct that can be practised rather than a slogan about humility.

👥 After noticing that you have belittled someone

Self-review becomes more concrete when it leads to repair. If we interrupted, embarrassed, ignored someone's contribution, or took credit that belonged elsewhere, choose one corrective action: apologize, restore recognition, make room for the person to speak, or change how feedback is given. The goal is not to perform humility publicly but to reduce the effect of behavior that has already occurred.

🪞 Reflection

What do my face and tone communicate to someone with less status?

If correction comes from someone I dislike, can I still assess whether it is true?

Has any advantage made me feel I no longer need to listen?

💡 Main Wisdom

Humility is not making oneself small; it is accepting truth and protecting another person's dignity even when we possess advantages.

✅ Practical Action Today

In one conversation today, practice humility: listen fully and do not use position, knowledge, wealth, or experience to diminish the other person.
