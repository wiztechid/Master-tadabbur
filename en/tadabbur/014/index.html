Quran verse about speaking kindly to others

SESSION 014 • AL-ISRA 17:53 • THEMATIC/NON-LINEAR

Choose the Better Words

Not every true sentence needs to be delivered in the sharpest possible way. The Qur'an directs Allah's servants not merely toward acceptable speech, but toward speech that is better—because a sentence can calm a problem or open another door to discord.

🌙 Today's Key Verse

وَقُل لِّعِبَادِى يَقُولُوا۟ ٱلَّتِى هِىَ أَحْسَنُ ۚ إِنَّ ٱلشَّيْطَـٰنَ يَنزَغُ بَيْنَهُمْ ۚ إِنَّ ٱلشَّيْطَـٰنَ كَانَ لِلْإِنسَـٰنِ عَدُوًّۭا مُّبِينًۭا
Wa qul li‘ibādī yaqūlullatī hiya aḥsanu, innasy-syaiṭāna yanzaghu bainahum, innasy-syaiṭāna kāna lil-insāni ‘aduwwan mubīnā.
Al-Isra 17:53 — Tell My bondmen to speak that which is kindlier. Lo! the devil soweth discord among them. Lo! the devil is for man an open foe.
English Qur'an translation reference: M. Pickthall (public-domain translation). Arabic text and transliteration are shared with the Indonesian edition. Transliteration is a reading aid and does not replace tajwid or talaqqi.

The verse connects the quality of speech with the quality of relationships. Conflict does not always begin with a major issue. Sometimes it grows because something that could have been said clearly and courteously was delivered in a needlessly harmful way.

Al-Isra 17:53

Being correct is not the whole task—choose a better way

The word aḥsan points toward what is better. This is not a command to blur the truth or constantly please other people. When several lawful ways of saying something are available, choose the one that preserves truth while reducing unnecessary harm.

In families, workplaces, message groups, public service, and social media, both the content and the manner of a message can have consequences.

📚 Tafsir Notes

Better words close a door to conflict

Ibn Kathir explains Al-Isra 17:53 as a command for believers to address one another with the best and most courteous words. Otherwise, Satan can stir discord until words develop into harmful actions, hostility, and conflict.

Good manners do not erase firmness

Choosing better speech does not mean concealing wrongdoing, avoiding advice, or tolerating injustice. Correction can remain firm and clear. What is restrained is humiliation, provocation, exaggeration, and language that is unnecessary for establishing what is right.

A pause creates room to choose

When emotions are high, our range of words narrows. A short pause creates space to ask: is this sentence necessary, true, beneficial, and expressed in the best way I can reasonably choose?

🤲 Hadith

In Sahih al-Bukhari 6136, the Prophet ﷺ connects belief in Allah and the Last Day with a simple but demanding principle: speak good or remain silent.

Silence here does not mean abandoning a duty to speak truth. It can serve as a brake when what is about to be said carries no worthwhile good.

🧭 Fact • Lesson • Boundary

Fact from revelation: Al-Isra 17:53 commands better speech and states that Satan sows discord; an authentic hadith directs believers to speak good or remain silent.

Lesson: communication is part of character. A true message can lose much of its benefit when it is deliberately delivered to demean, provoke, or shame.

Boundary: this verse does not prohibit criticism, correction, reporting wrongdoing, testimony, or necessary factual disclosure. Good speech is not the same as weakness. No prediction about a modern event is drawn from this verse.

🪞 Reflection

In my most recent conflict, did the problem grow because of the substance—or because of the way I delivered it?

Is there one sentence I could still make firm without diminishing the person who needs to hear it?

💡 Wisdom

Truth requires courage to be spoken; character requires wisdom in choosing how it is spoken.

✅ Practical Action Today

Before sending one important message or giving one response likely to trigger emotion, read it once more. Remove one word that serves only to mock, belittle, or prolong conflict—without weakening the truth that genuinely needs to be said.
