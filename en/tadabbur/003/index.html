Why Did Allah Create Us According to the Quran? — Quran 2:21–22

SESSION 003 • AL-BAQARAH 21–22

What Were We Created For?

From recognizing the Creator toward worship, gratitude, tawhid, and taqwa.

🌙 Today’s Key Verse

يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّاسُ ٱعْبُدُوا۟ رَبَّكُمُ ٱلَّذِى خَلَقَكُمْ وَٱلَّذِينَ مِن قَبْلِكُمْ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَتَّقُونَ
Yā ayyuhan-nāsu‘budū rabbakumullażī khalaqakum wallażīna min qablikum la‘allakum tattaqūn.
Al-Baqarah 2:21 — O mankind! Worship your Lord, Who hath created you and those before you, so that ye may ward off (evil).

English Qur'an translation reference: M. Pickthall. Arabic text and transliteration follow the published Indonesian edition. Transliteration is a reading aid and does not replace tajwid or talaqqi.

Life does not begin with “what do I want?” but with a more fundamental question: Who created me, and toward Whom is this life directed?

Al-Baqarah 21

A call to all humanity

يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّاسُ ٱعْبُدُوا۟ رَبَّكُمُ ٱلَّذِى خَلَقَكُمْ وَٱلَّذِينَ مِن قَبْلِكُمْ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَتَّقُونَ
Yā ayyuhan-nāsu‘budū rabbakumullażī khalaqakum wallażīna min qablikum la‘allakum tattaqūn.
O mankind! Worship your Lord, Who hath created you and those before you, so that ye may ward off (evil).

The address is “O mankind,” making the message fundamental and universal. Allah links the command to worship with the reality that He is the Lord and Creator. The stated aim is clear: that you may attain taqwa.

Al-Baqarah 22

Reading blessings as signs

ٱلَّذِى جَعَلَ لَكُمُ ٱلْأَرْضَ فِرَٰشًا وَٱلسَّمَآءَ بِنَآءً وَأَنزَلَ مِنَ ٱلسَّمَآءِ مَآءً فَأَخْرَجَ بِهِۦ مِنَ ٱلثَّمَرَٰتِ رِزْقًا لَّكُمْ ۖ فَلَا تَجْعَلُوا۟ لِلَّهِ أَندَادًا وَأَنتُمْ تَعْلَمُونَ
Allażī ja‘ala lakumul-arḍa firāsyan was-samā’a binā’an wa anzala minas-samā’i mā’an fa akhraja bihī minaṡ-ṡamarāti rizqal lakum, fa lā taj‘alū lillāhi andādaw wa antum ta‘lamūn.
Who hath appointed the earth a resting-place for you, and the sky a canopy; and causeth water to pour down from the sky, thereby producing fruits as food for you. And do not set up rivals to Allah when ye know (better).

Earth, sky, water, and provision are not presented merely as information about nature. The verse directs observation toward tawhid: blessings should lead us to know and serve the Giver of those blessings.

📚 Tafsir Mu’tabar

Worship and tawhid

Classical commentators such as Ibn Kathir explain that the One who created humanity and provides the means of life is the One who alone deserves worship. Tawhid is more than acknowledging a Creator; it means directing worship only to Him.

From blessing to taqwa

The sequence matters: humanity is reminded of creation and blessings, then commanded to worship, and then directed toward taqwa. Blessings do not end in comfort; they create moral responsibility before the Giver.

📜 Relevant Hadith

Allah looks at hearts and deeds

In Sahih Muslim, it is reported that Allah does not look at people’s outward forms and wealth, but at their hearts and deeds. Servitude is therefore more than identity or appearance; the orientation of the heart should appear in action.

Allah’s right over His servants

In the hadith of Mu‘adh ibn Jabal recorded by al-Bukhari and Muslim, the Prophet ﷺ explains that Allah’s right over His servants is that they worship Him and do not associate anything with Him. This closely matches Al-Baqarah 21–22.

🌍 Fact × Lesson × Prediction Boundary

Fact: humans can develop technology, economic systems, organizations, and resource management, yet still live through realities they did not create themselves—water, soil, ecosystems, the body, and the laws of nature.

Lesson/analogy: work, office, money, technology, networks, and personal ability are means. They matter, but they must not take Allah’s place in the heart as though they were the ultimate source of safety.

Boundary: Al-Baqarah 21–22 are not specific predictions about food crises, climate change, disasters, or particular modern events. Their contemporary relevance is as principles of tawhid, gratitude, responsibility, and awareness of human limits.

💭 Reflection

What most easily grows too large in my heart—money, position, other people’s approval, my own ability, or a sense of security from systems—until dependence upon Allah begins to shrink?

✨ Practical Action Today

Choose one blessing that usually feels ordinary—water, food, health, work, family, the ability to think, or time. Pause to recognize where it comes from, give thanks, then prove that gratitude through one concrete action pleasing to Allah.
