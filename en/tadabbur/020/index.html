Quran verse about being just to people you dislike

SESSION 020 • AL-MA'IDAH 5:8 • THEMATIC/NON-LINEAR

Remain Just Even toward Those You Dislike

Justice is easiest when we like the person being judged. The harder test comes when there is hurt, conflict, disagreement, or dislike. The Qur'an commands us not to let hatred move us away from justice.

🌙 Today's Key Verse

يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ كُونُوا۟ قَوَّٰمِينَ لِلَّهِ شُهَدَآءَ بِٱلْقِسْطِ ۖ وَلَا يَجْرِمَنَّكُمْ شَنَـَٔانُ قَوْمٍ عَلَىٰٓ أَلَّا تَعْدِلُوا۟ ۚ ٱعْدِلُوا۟ هُوَ أَقْرَبُ لِلتَّقْوَىٰ ۖ وَٱتَّقُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ ۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ خَبِيرٌۢ بِمَا تَعْمَلُونَ
Yā ayyuhallażīna āmanū kūnū qawwāmīna lillāhi syuhadā'a bil-qisṭi, wa lā yajrimannakum syana'ānu qawmin ‘alā allā ta‘dilū. I‘dilū huwa aqrabu lit-taqwā, wattaqullāh. Innallāha khabīrun bimā ta‘malūn.
Al-Ma'idah 5:8 — M. Pickthall: O ye who believe! Be steadfast witnesses for Allah in equity, and let not hatred of any people seduce you that ye deal not justly. Deal justly, that is nearer to your duty. Observe your duty to Allah. Lo! Allah is Informed of what ye do.
English Qur'an translation reference: M. Pickthall (public-domain translation). Arabic text and transliteration are identical to the Indonesian edition. Transliteration is a reading aid and does not replace tajwid or talaqqi.

The verse does not ask us to erase moral judgment about wrongdoing. It demands something more disciplined: dislike must not alter the standard of justice.

📚 Tafsir Notes

Justice applies to friend and opponent alike

In his tafsir of Al-Ma'idah 5:8, Ibn Kathir explains that hatred of a people must not cause a person to abandon justice. Justice is to be upheld with everyone, whether friend or enemy.

Justice is nearer to taqwa

The verse connects justice with taqwa. Justice is therefore not merely a legal or administrative technique; it is discipline of the heart before Allah. When emotion has a reason to take sides, taqwa keeps the measure from changing.

🤲 Authentic Hadith

In Sahih al-Bukhari 2444, the Prophet ﷺ commanded Muslims to help a brother whether he is an oppressor or oppressed. When the Companions asked how an oppressor should be helped, he explained that the help is to prevent him from oppressing others. True loyalty does not mean validating the wrongdoing of someone we support.

🧭 Fact • Lesson • Boundary

Fact from revelation: Al-Ma'idah 5:8 explicitly forbids hatred of a people from becoming a reason to abandon justice. Ibn Kathir emphasizes justice toward friend and opponent alike.

Lesson: integrity becomes visible when the standard we use for people we like remains the same when applied to people we dislike.

Boundary: justice does not mean treating right and wrong as equal, erasing consequences, or tolerating violations. Justice may require correction, lawful sanctions, protection of victims, and stopping oppression—but these must follow a sound standard rather than revenge.

🌍 Factual Context

This principle is highly relevant in families, workplaces, organizations, economic dealings, rule enforcement, and government. Conflicts of interest, personal closeness, rivalry, and group sentiment can distort judgment. Procedures, evidence, documentation, a right of response, separation of authority, and consistent standards can help people protect decisions from personal bias.

Lesson/analogy: good systems help us remain fair when our emotions are not neutral. This is an application of the principle, not a claim that the verse predicts a particular modern administrative system.

Prediction: no specific modern event is predicted by this verse.

🪞 Reflection

Does my standard change when the same mistake is made by someone I like and someone I dislike?

In one conflict I am facing, which parts are facts and rights, and which parts are simply the urge to retaliate?

💡 Main Wisdom

The most honest test of justice often comes not when we are neutral, but when the heart has a reason to take sides.

✅ Practical Action Today

Choose one judgment or decision involving someone you like or dislike. Before deciding, write three things: verified facts, the standard that should apply to anyone, and the personal emotion that must be separated. Then apply the same standard.
