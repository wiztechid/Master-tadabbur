SESSION 016 • AL-HASHR 59:18 • THEMATIC/NON-LINEAR

Audit Yourself Before the Day Ends

The Qur'an does not only ask us to look at what has happened. It asks us to look at what we are sending ahead. Self-accountability turns today into preparation for the most certain tomorrow.

🌙 Today's Key Verse

يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ ٱتَّقُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ وَلْتَنظُرْ نَفْسٌۭ مَّا قَدَّمَتْ لِغَدٍۢ ۖ وَٱتَّقُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ ۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ خَبِيرٌۢ بِمَا تَعْمَلُونَ
Yā ayyuhallażīna āmanuttaqullāha waltanẓur nafsun mā qaddamat lighad, wattaqullāh, innallāha khabīrun bimā ta‘malūn.
Al-Hashr 59:18 — M. Pickthall: O ye who believe! Observe your duty to Allah. And let every soul look to that which it sendeth on before for the morrow. And observe your duty to Allah! Lo! Allah is Informed of what ye do.
English Qur'an translation reference: M. Pickthall (public-domain translation). Arabic text and transliteration are shared with the Indonesian edition.

“The morrow” in the verse points to the life to come. Self-accountability is therefore more than a productivity technique; it is an examination of life's direction before Allah.

📚 Tafsir Notes

Look at what you have sent ahead

Ibn Kathir explains this command as a call to hold ourselves accountable and consider the good deeds prepared for the Day of return to Allah. Taqwa appears before and after the command to reflect: sound self-review does not end in guilt; it redirects obedience.

Reflection should become action

In the report cited with this verse, the Prophet ﷺ urged charity when people in visible need arrived, and the Companions responded. The lesson is practical: thinking about what is sent ahead should move from reflection into deeds today.

🤲 Hadith

In Sahih Muslim 1017a, when people in severe need came, the Prophet ﷺ encouraged charity and the Companions followed one another in giving. Ibn Kathir connects this report with Al-Hashr 59:18, showing how awareness of the Hereafter can produce concrete action.

🧭 Fact • Lesson • Boundary

Fact from revelation: Allah commands taqwa and every soul to examine what it has sent ahead for “tomorrow”.

Lesson: healthy self-review asks not only “what did I achieve?” but also “what good did I send ahead, what rights did I fulfill, and what wrong still needs repair?”

Boundary: this verse is not a prediction about a modern event and is not an invitation to drown in guilt. Self-accountability should lead toward repentance, repair, and real action.

🪞 Reflection

If today were a page I would see again before Allah, what part would I be most grateful for?

What one right, mistake, or good deed can I still address before this day truly ends?

💡 Wisdom

Do not only count what you gained today; also look at what you sent ahead for the Hereafter.

✅ Practical Action Today

Before sleep, make a three-line audit: Gratitude — one blessing consciously recognized; Istighfar — one wrong to repent from or repair; Provision — one small good deed you can still do or turn into a concrete next step.
