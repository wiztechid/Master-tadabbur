Quran 83:1–3: Do Not Give People Less Than Their Due

SESSION 021 • AL-MUTAFFIFIN 83:1–3 • THEMATIC/NON-LINEAR

Do Not Give People Less Than Their Due

Dishonesty does not always appear as obvious theft. It can appear when a person demands his own rights in full, yet reduces the measure, quality, time, information, or obligation owed when it is his turn to fulfill another person's rights.

Quick answer: what does Quran 83:1–3 condemn?

Quran 83:1–3 condemns those who take their own measure in full but give others less by measure or weight. Its direct context is commercial measurement; the broader moral lesson is not to demand one standard for yourself while giving another person less than what is due.

🌙 Today's Key Verse

وَيْلٌۭ لِّلْمُطَفِّفِينَ ۝ ٱلَّذِينَ إِذَا ٱكْتَالُوا۟ عَلَى ٱلنَّاسِ يَسْتَوْفُونَ ۝ وَإِذَا كَالُوهُمْ أَو وَّزَنُوهُمْ يُخْسِرُونَ
Wailul lil-muṭaffifīn. Allażīna iżaktālū ‘alan-nāsi yastaufūn. Wa iżā kālūhum au wazanūhum yukhsirūn.
Al-Mutaffifin 83:1–3 — M. Pickthall: Woe unto the defrauders: Those who when they take the measure from mankind demand it full, But if they measure unto them or weigh for them, they cause them loss.
English Qur'an translation reference: M. Pickthall (public-domain translation). Arabic text and transliteration are identical to the Indonesian edition. Transliteration is a reading aid and does not replace tajwid or talaqqi.

The verses expose a moral imbalance that is close to everyday life: a full standard for oneself, but a lower standard for others. The Qur'an addresses not only the number on a scale, but the character behind the fraud.

📚 Tafsir Notes

Who are al-muṭaffifīn?

In Ibn Kathir's tafsir of Al-Mutaffifin 83:1–3, tatfif is explained as giving short measure or weight. Those condemned take their own due from others in full but diminish what they owe when measuring for others.

The prohibition protects fairness in transactions

Al-Tabari explains the condemnation of those who diminish people's rights in measure and weight. The principle requires honesty both when receiving and when delivering a due, rather than changing the standard according to who benefits.

A broader moral lesson without erasing the original context

The direct context is measure and weight. As a lesson, the same pattern can be recognized when someone demands another person's obligations in full while deliberately reducing his own. This is an analogy in applying the principle, not a replacement of the verse's original meaning.

🤲 Authentic Hadith

In Sahih Muslim 102, the Prophet ﷺ passed a pile of food offered for sale. When he found wet food hidden underneath, he instructed that it should have been placed where buyers could see it, and taught that whoever deceives is not of him.

The hadith adds an important dimension to fair dealing: not only must the quantity be truthful, but information that affects another party's rights must not be deliberately concealed.

🌍 Factual Context

Fact from revelation: Al-Mutaffifin 83:1–3 condemns giving short measure or weight when fulfilling other people's rights, while authentic hadith prohibits deception in transactions.

Lesson/analogy: the principle is relevant to trade, work, public service, contracts, procurement, reporting, and social relations: do not demand full output from others while deliberately reducing your own obligation. In government, for example, accuracy of quantity, quality, time, documentation, and payment are areas where verifiable standards matter.

Boundary: the verses do not point to a particular modern administrative system and are not a prediction about a specific economic case. Modern application must still follow the relevant facts, agreements, rules, and evidence.

🪞 Reflection

Am I more careful when calculating what is owed to me than when calculating what I owe to others?

In my most recent work or transaction, is there any quality, time, information, or obligation that I expect fully from others but have not yet delivered by the same standard myself?

💡 Main Wisdom

Integrity appears when the measure we demand for ourselves is also the measure we use when fulfilling other people's rights.

✅ Practical Action Today

Choose one obligation involving another person's right—a payment, work product, service, promised time, product quality, or information. Check it once more before delivery: are the quantity, quality, timing, and disclosure as honest as the standard you would expect if you were the recipient?
