Quran Verse About Trust and Justice in Leadership — Quran 4:58

SESSION 004 • THEMATIC/NON-LINEAR • AN-NISA 58 & 135

Trust Does Not End with Office

Trust → returning rights to their owners → justice in decisions → courage to be just even against oneself.

🌙 Today’s Key Verse

إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَأْمُرُكُمْ أَن تُؤَدُّوا۟ ٱلْأَمَٰنَٰتِ إِلَىٰٓ أَهْلِهَا وَإِذَا حَكَمْتُم بَيْنَ ٱلنَّاسِ أَن تَحْكُمُوا۟ بِٱلْعَدْلِ ۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ نِعِمَّا يَعِظُكُم بِهِۦٓ ۗ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ كَانَ سَمِيعًۢا بَصِيرًا
Innallāha ya’murukum an tu’addul-amānāti ilā ahlihā, wa iżā ḥakamtum bainan-nāsi an taḥkumū bil-‘adl. Innallāha ni‘immā ya‘iẓukum bih. Innallāha kāna samī‘am baṣīrā.
An-Nisa 4:58 — Lo! Allah commandeth you that ye restore deposits to their owners, and, if ye judge between mankind, that ye judge justly. Lo! comely is this which Allah admonisheth you. Lo! Allah is ever Hearer, Seer.

English Qur'an translation reference: M. Pickthall. Arabic text and transliteration follow the published Indonesian edition. Transliteration is a reading aid and does not replace tajwid or talaqqi.

In the Qur’an, amanah is not merely “do not steal.” It means placing things where their rights belong: other people’s rights, decisions, information, wealth, time, authority, and even the judgments we make.

An-Nisa 58

Trust must return to the one entitled to it

This verse joins two matters: trust and justice. Whenever a person holds something that is not wholly their own—office, organizational funds, data, decisions, working time, authority, or confidence—they are carrying a trust.

Ibn Kathir explains that the command to return trusts is broad, not restricted to a particular group. The verse is also a strong basis for those with authority to decide fairly.

An-Nisa 135

Justice is tested when it touches our own interests

يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ كُونُوا۟ قَوَّٰمِينَ بِٱلْقِسْطِ شُهَدَآءَ لِلَّهِ وَلَوْ عَلَىٰٓ أَنفُسِكُمْ أَوِ ٱلْوَٰلِدَيْنِ وَٱلْأَقْرَبِينَ ۚ إِن يَكُنْ غَنِيًّا أَوْ فَقِيرًا فَٱللَّهُ أَوْلَىٰ بِهِمَا ۖ فَلَا تَتَّبِعُوا۟ ٱلْهَوَىٰٓ أَن تَعْدِلُوا۟ ۚ وَإِن تَلْوُۥٓا۟ أَوْ تُعْرِضُوا۟ فَإِنَّ ٱللَّهَ كَانَ بِمَا تَعْمَلُونَ خَبِيرًا
Yā ayyuhallażīna āmanū kūnū qawwāmīna bil-qisṭi syuhadā’a lillāhi walau ‘alā anfusikum awil-wālidaini wal-aqrabīn. Iy yakun ganiyyan aw faqīran fallāhu awlā bihimā, fa lā tattabi‘ul-hawā an ta‘dilū. Wa in talwū aw tu‘riḍū fa innallāha kāna bimā ta‘malūna khabīrā.
O ye who believe! Be ye staunch in justice, witnesses for Allah, even though it be against yourselves or (your) parents or (your) kindred, whether (the case be of) a rich man or a poor man, for Allah is nearer unto both (than ye are). So follow not passion lest ye lapse (from truth), and if ye lapse or fall away, then lo! Allah is ever Informed of what ye do.

Easy justice does not necessarily prove integrity. The real test comes when the right decision works against ourselves, someone close to us, our group, or a person we prefer.

📚 Tafsir Mu’tabar

The broad meaning of trust

Ibn Kathir explains An-Nisa 58 as a general command: trusts must be delivered to their owners. Amanah includes rights owed to Allah and rights owed to people, making it wider than the return of physical property.

Justice and authority

The command to judge people with justice is directly relevant to anyone who assesses, decides, allocates, approves, or determines another person’s rights.

Desire as a threat to justice

An-Nisa 135 makes clear that wealth, poverty, family ties, and self-interest must not change the standard of truth. Justice follows what can be responsibly justified, not whom we like.

📜 Relevant Hadith

“Every one of you is a shepherd…”

The Prophet ﷺ taught that every person is a caretaker and will be questioned about what is under their care. This is recorded in Sahih al-Bukhari 7138 and Sahih Muslim 1829a. Accountability does not begin only when someone becomes a senior leader; every sphere of responsibility creates an amanah.

🌍 Fact × Lesson × Prediction Boundary

Fact: almost everyone carries some form of trust—parents toward family, employees toward time and tasks, treasurers toward money, supervisors toward evaluations, public officers toward service, and officials toward public authority.

Lesson: greater authority creates greater potential for benefit and greater risk of injustice. Integrity therefore requires more than good intentions; it needs procedure, evidence, consistency, and courage to apply the same standard without favoritism.

Boundary: these verses are not predictions about a particular government, legal case, or public figure. They state a timeless moral principle: trusts must be fulfilled and justice must not submit to desire.

💭 Reflection

Is there a decision I treat differently merely because it concerns myself, someone close to me, someone I like, or someone I dislike?

If everyone affected could see the process behind my decision, would I still feel at peace that their rights were treated fairly?

✨ Practical Action Today

Choose one real trust in your hands—work, money, a document, time, an evaluation, a family decision, or even a small authority. Ask: who owns the right, what is the fair standard, and is my personal interest distorting the decision? Correct one thing if needed.
