Quran 4:29: Trade by Mutual Consent

SESSION 025 • AN-NISA 4:29 • THEMATIC/NON-LINEAR

Do Not Take Rights Without Mutual Consent

Transactions, consent, wealth, rights.

🌙 Today's Key Verse

يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ لَا تَأْكُلُوٓا۟ أَمْوَٰلَكُم بَيْنَكُم بِٱلْبَـٰطِلِ إِلَّآ أَن تَكُونَ تِجَـٰرَةً عَن تَرَاضٍۢ مِّنكُمْ ۚ وَلَا تَقْتُلُوٓا۟ أَنفُسَكُمْ ۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ كَانَ بِكُمْ رَحِيمًۭا
Yā ayyuhallażīna āmanū lā ta’kulū amwālakum bainakum bil-bāṭili illā an takūna tijāratan ‘an tarāḍim minkum, wa lā taqtulū anfusakum. Innallāha kāna bikum raḥīmā.
AN-NISA 4:29 — M. Pickthall: O ye who believe! Squander not your wealth among yourselves in vanity, except it be a trade by mutual consent, and kill not one another. Lo! Allah is ever Merciful unto you.
English Qur'an translation reference: M. Pickthall (public-domain translation). Arabic text and transliteration are identical to the Indonesian edition. Transliteration is a reading aid and does not replace tajwid or talaqqi.

📚 Tafsir Notes

Wealth must not move through wrongful means

Ibn Kathir explains the prohibition broadly in relation to ways of acquiring wealth that Allah has forbidden. The concern is not merely the label of a transaction, but the path by which wealth is obtained.

Consent must be meaningful

Al-Tabari presents trade by mutual consent as a permitted path in contrast with wrongful appropriation. Agreement should not become a cover for deception or coercion.

Boundary

The verse gives a revealed moral principle, not an automatic ruling on every modern dispute. Object, terms, authority, facts, fiqh, and applicable law still matter.

🤲 Authentic Hadith

Sahih al-Bukhari 2079 teaches that truthfulness and disclosure bring blessing to a sale, while lying and concealment erase its blessing.

🌍 Factual Context

Fact from revelation: the verse prohibits wrongful consumption of wealth and identifies trade by mutual consent.

Lesson/analogy: material terms should not be hidden and stronger bargaining power should not become a tool for taking another person's rights.

Boundary: consent does not make every object or method automatically lawful.

🤝 Consent is more than saying “yes”

In everyday life, agreement can appear to exist even when someone does not fully understand what they are accepting. A person may agree out of embarrassment, fear of losing an opportunity, a sense that there is no real choice, or because an important condition appears only after the decision. Before a transaction or agreement, pause and ask whether material information is visible, whether the other person has room to ask questions, and whether the decision is being made without pressure that disguises the absence of choice.

This can arise in online purchases, personal loans, shared family expenses, extra work, or changes to an existing arrangement. Transparency cannot guarantee that everyone will be satisfied, but it reduces the risk of obtaining agreement by withholding information that matters.

🧭 A Consent Check before agreeing

Use four practical questions. What exactly is being agreed? Clarify the subject, price, duties, timing, and relevant consequences. What remains unknown? Do not hide material facts simply because the other person did not ask. Is there pressure? Distinguish ordinary persuasion from threats, manipulation, or exploitation of dependency. Can each side explain the agreement back? If both can describe its core terms in their own words, misunderstanding is less likely.

This checklist is an editorial caution tool, not a fiqh formula that decides whether every contract is valid or invalid. Particular transactions can involve more specific Islamic and legal rules and may require appropriate expert guidance.

🏠 When bargaining power is unequal

Consent deserves extra attention when one side holds much more power, information, or control over a needed resource. A manager and employee, a lender and someone in distress, or family members with unequal financial control may not be deciding from equal positions. Greater power does not automatically mean wrongdoing; the practical question is whether that advantage is used to hide information or force a decision that should have room for consideration.

Giving time to read, explaining choices in plain language, providing a copy of the agreement, and not humiliating someone for asking questions are practical ways to reduce avoidable pressure.

🚧 Scope boundary

This session does not issue a ruling on the validity of a particular contract and does not replace qualified advice for complex transactions. Its focus is the ethical habit before saying “agreed”: transparency, understanding, room to choose, and responsibility toward another person's rights. Session 022 remains focused on wrongful consumption of wealth, while Session 024 focuses on honoring commitments already made. This session owns the question of how meaningful consent is formed.

🪞 Reflection

Is there an agreement that looks consensual while an important term remains poorly understood?

When I have more information or bargaining power, do I use it to preserve fairness?

Is there a small right belonging to someone else that I have treated as insignificant?

💡 Main Wisdom

Lawful wealth is not only about what we possess, but how it came into our hands. Consent, truthfulness, and respect for rights are part of integrity.

✅ Practical Action Today

Review one transaction or agreement today: make sure the other party understands the material terms and truly consents without manipulation.
