SESSION 007 • THEMATIC/NON-LINEAR • AZ-ZUMAR 53 & AT-TAHRIM 8

Do Not Despair of Returning

Sin must not be treated lightly, but it must not become a reason to stay away from Allah. The road home is repentance.

🌙 Today’s Key Verse

قُلْ يَـٰعِبَادِىَ ٱلَّذِينَ أَسْرَفُوا۟ عَلَىٰٓ أَنفُسِهِمْ لَا تَقْنَطُوا۟ مِن رَّحْمَةِ ٱللَّهِ ۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَغْفِرُ ٱلذُّنُوبَ جَمِيعًا ۚ إِنَّهُۥ هُوَ ٱلْغَفُورُ ٱلرَّحِيمُ
Qul yā ‘ibādiyallażīna asrafū ‘alā anfusihim lā taqnaṭū mir raḥmatillāh. Innallāha yagfiruż-żunūba jamī‘ā. Innahū huwal-gafūrur-raḥīm.
Az-Zumar 39:53 — Say: O My slaves who have been prodigal to their own hurt! Despair not of the mercy of Allah, Who forgiveth all sins. Lo! He is the Forgiving, the Merciful.

English Qur'an translation reference: M. Pickthall. Arabic text and transliteration follow the published Indonesian edition. Transliteration is a reading aid and does not replace tajwid or talaqqi.

The verse does not minimize sin. It addresses people who have exceeded limits against themselves—yet Allah still tells them: do not despair of His mercy.

Az-Zumar 53

Despair can also keep us away from the road home

Ibn Kathir explains this verse as a call to sinners to repent and return to Allah. Its vast promise of forgiveness does not mean one may continue sinning without repentance; the context is an invitation to return before the opportunity ends.

The past cannot be changed, but what we do after recognizing a wrong can change. Healthy guilt pushes toward repair; guilt that becomes despair can stop a person from returning.

At-Tahrim 8

Repentance is not only regret — it changes direction

يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ تُوبُوٓا۟ إِلَى ٱللَّهِ تَوْبَةًۭ نَّصُوحًا عَسَىٰ رَبُّكُمْ أَن يُكَفِّرَ عَنكُمْ سَيِّـَٔاتِكُمْ وَيُدْخِلَكُمْ جَنَّـٰتٍۢ تَجْرِى مِن تَحْتِهَا ٱلْأَنْهَـٰرُ يَوْمَ لَا يُخْزِى ٱللَّهُ ٱلنَّبِىَّ وَٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ مَعَهُۥ ۖ نُورُهُمْ يَسْعَىٰ بَيْنَ أَيْدِيهِمْ وَبِأَيْمَـٰنِهِمْ يَقُولُونَ رَبَّنَآ أَتْمِمْ لَنَا نُورَنَا وَٱغْفِرْ لَنَآ ۖ إِنَّكَ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَىْءٍۢ قَدِيرٌ
Yā ayyuhallażīna āmanū tūbū ilallāhi taubatan naṣūḥā. ‘Asā rabbukum ay yukaffira ‘ankum sayyi’ātikum wa yudkhilakum jannātin tajrī min taḥtihal-anhāru yauma lā yukhzillāhun-nabiyya wallażīna āmanū ma‘ah. Nūruhum yas‘ā baina aidīhim wa bi-aimānihim, yaqūlūna rabbanā atmim lanā nūranā wagfir lanā, innaka ‘alā kulli syai’in qadīr.
O ye who believe! Turn unto Allah in sincere repentance! It may be that your Lord will remit from you your evil deeds and bring you into Gardens underneath which rivers flow, on the day when Allah will not abase the Prophet and those who believe with him. Their light will run before them and on their right hands; they will say: Our Lord! Perfect our light for us, and forgive us! Lo! Thou art Able to do all things.

Ibn Kathir explains taubatan naṣūḥā as sincere, decisive repentance that removes the previous evil and restrains a person from continuing in the same destructive pattern.

📚 Tafsir Mu’tabar

Allah’s mercy must not become an excuse to trivialize sin

Az-Zumar 53 is a door of hope, not a license to keep sinning. Ibn Kathir emphasizes that it is a call to repent and return. Two truths therefore stay together: do not make light of sin, and do not make light of Allah’s vast mercy.

What does sincere repentance mean?

At-Tahrim 8 commands taubatan naṣūḥā. Classical explanations describe sincere repentance as leaving the sin, regretting it, and firmly intending not to return. When another person’s rights are involved, repair also requires addressing those rights according to one’s ability and the proper rules.

📜 Authentic Hadith

The door of repentance remains open

The Prophet ﷺ explained that Allah opens the opportunity for repentance at night for the one who sinned by day, and by day for the one who sinned at night, until the major sign mentioned in the hadith occurs. Sahih Muslim 2759a–b.

The message is not “repent later,” but the opposite: while the door remains open, do not delay returning.

🌍 Fact × Lesson × Prediction Boundary

Fact: people can repeat mistakes in speech, work, relationships, money, decisions, and private habits. Recognizing wrongdoing often brings shame and guilt.

Lesson: guilt is useful when it leads to admission, repair, restoration of rights, and changed behavior. “I am too bad to change” is not humility when it becomes an excuse to avoid a repair that is still possible.

Boundary: repentance does not promise that every worldly consequence disappears. Social, legal, health, financial, or interpersonal consequences may still need to be addressed.

💭 Reflection

Is there a mistake I keep carrying as a reason to stay far from Allah when it should instead become a reason to return to Him?

If the mistake involved another person: what concrete repair can I still make?

✨ Core Wisdom

A believer does not choose between fearing sin and hoping in mercy. Both are needed. Fear stops us from trivializing sin; hope stops us from giving up on returning to Allah.

✅ Practical Action Today

Take five minutes after one prayer. Name before Allah one mistake you truly want to stop. Make conscious istighfar, identify one trigger to avoid, and take one repair step today. If another person’s right remains unresolved, write the first step toward resolving it.
